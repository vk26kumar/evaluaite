# Physics · Class 10: Unit Test: Electricity

20 marks · 45 minutes. Copy each question and model answer into the Grade page, with the marks shown.

## Question 1 (1 mark)

**Question:** The SI unit of electric resistance is: Options: (A) volt, (B) ampere, (C) ohm, (D) watt.

**Model answer:**

(C) ohm

## Question 2 (1 mark)

**Question:** Which instrument is used to measure electric current in a circuit? Options: (A) voltmeter, (B) ammeter, (C) galvanometer, (D) potentiometer.

**Model answer:**

(B) ammeter

## Question 3 (2 marks)

**Question:** State Ohm's law.

**Model answer:**

- At constant temperature, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends (1 mark).
- Written as V = IR, where R is the resistance (1 mark).

## Question 4 (2 marks)

**Question:** Define electric power and give its SI unit.

**Model answer:**

- Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is consumed or work is done, P = VI (1 mark).
- Its SI unit is the watt, W (1 mark).

## Question 5 (3 marks)

**Question:** A 12 V battery is connected across a 4 Ω resistor. Find the current in the circuit and the power used by the resistor.

**Model answer:**

I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A (1.5 marks).
P = VI = 12 × 3 = 36 W (1.5 marks).

## Question 6 (3 marks)

**Question:** Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected (a) in series and (b) in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance in each case.

**Model answer:**

(a) Series: R = 2 + 3 + 6 = 11 Ω (1.5 marks).
(b) Parallel: 1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1, so R = 1 Ω (1.5 marks).

## Question 7 (4 marks)

**Question:** Why are household appliances connected in parallel and not in series? Give three reasons.

**Model answer:**

- Each appliance gets the full mains voltage of 220 V (about 1.5 marks).
- If one appliance is switched off or fails, the others keep working (about 1.5 marks).
- Each appliance draws current according to its own resistance and can have its own switch and fuse (1 mark).

## Question 8 (4 marks)

**Question:** Explain the heating effect of electric current. State Joule's law of heating and give two applications.

**Model answer:**

- Current through a resistor converts electrical energy into heat (1 mark).
- Joule's law: H = I²Rt, heat is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance and the time (2 marks).
- Two applications, e.g. electric iron, heater or geyser, filament bulb, fuse (1 mark).
